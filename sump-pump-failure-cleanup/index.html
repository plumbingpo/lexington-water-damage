Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Lexington

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Do not wait for the water to drain away by itself.
What We Do

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Get the Water Out

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Wash and Disinfect

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Stored Belongings

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.
